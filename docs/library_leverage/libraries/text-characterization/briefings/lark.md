# Lark: source-aligned characterization beyond mathematical parsing

**Discovery date: 2026-11-10.** Repository baseline: `0da4f8e614b162b791ac19dcb63f6cd33178377b`. All proposed directions below carry the design-evidence label **Proposed**. Source observations establish available mechanisms and current wiring; they establish neither application efficacy nor complete qualification.

This contribution considers the full objective: characterize fundamental textual and formal units upstream, in as many justified passes as needed, then combine their evidence into conceptual relationships, groups, labels and analytics. It does not constrain that inquiry to the present PM2 implementation footprint. Lark is promising as a family of bounded recognizers and structure processors within that larger system. It does not supply general natural-language understanding, concept identity or scientific truth.

The entire [Lark overview and addendum](../../../../libraries/parsing/lark.md) was read. Consequential contracts were checked against installed Lark 1.3.1 source under `.venv/lib/python3.14/site-packages/lark/`; selected tagged primary sources were also consulted. The brief supplies installed Python 3.14.10 and SymPy 1.14.0; the [application manifest](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/pyproject.toml) selects Lark 1.3.1, SymPy 1.14.0 and spaCy 3.8.18. No installation, behavioral probe, test, product-source mutation or qualification was performed. Concurrent evidence under `2026-11-10_schema-diagnostic-failure-boundaries` was untouched. No claim that a capability is unused across every possible dynamic path follows from the representative reads.

## Present capability footprint

The [math grammar owner](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/math/grammar.py) already exercises much of Lark's generalized parsing surface. `grammar_text()` combines SymPy's unedited shipped grammar, [local extensions](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/math/extension.lark), generated macro terminals and a controlled ignore list. `parser_for()` explicitly selects Earley, dynamic lexing, explicit ambiguity, positional propagation, all tokens, stable ordering and five typed starts. Its `edit_terminals=whole_name` prevents partial control-word matches. This is substantive integration, rather than simply calling a stock LaTeX parser.

The extension makes scripts, sets, function signatures, conditionals, cases, aligned equations and prose connectives inspectable syntax. Recognition already exceeds the subset representable as native SymPy values. `syntax()` retains positioned alternatives; `interpret()` applies declared roles; native equivalence is explicitly stronger. Function/product and tuple/interval ambiguities are not permission to guess. [PreparedMathInput](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/math/prepared.py) shares lexical capture, structure, syntax and interpretation within an operation, requests symbolic/scientific stages separately and avoids retaining timeouts as reusable conclusions. The current reusable preparation is therefore a useful foundation to preserve or deliberately supersede coherently, rather than duplicate.

There is also an important nonmath foothold. [semantic/structural_parsing.py](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/semantic/structural_parsing.py) prepares a basic lexer with `parser=None`, a contextual LALR threshold grammar and a literal grammar for explicitly labeled dates, identifiers, quantities and ranges. `threshold_fields()` nominates local spans before parsing complete fields. Dates and interval validity receive additional Python checks; grammar membership alone is insufficient.

Actual consumers include [predicate_contributions.py](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/predicate_contributions.py), [methods/structural_products.py](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/structural_products.py), and [semantic/structural_products.py](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/semantic/structural_products.py). The last checks typed premises and formal operands against exact located syntax. This means changing recognition, output identity or grammar versions affects producer and validation consumers together. Current field parsing is useful behavior, but it is not comprehensive textual characterization.

The [D4 progressive composition owner](../../../../../docs/design/D4-method-catalog.md) separates retained annotations, native mathematical syntax, interpretation and diagnostic retention. Its current semantic boundary supports a broader investigation: Lark can contribute source-grounded structural observations while later owners decide applicability, relationships, identity and public standing.

## Proposed directions

### 1. Extend lexical characterization and nonmath microgrammars

**Mechanism.** Reuse `Lark(..., parser=None, lexer="basic")` for a declared lexical vocabulary; combine independent region nomination with whole-region LALR/contextual recognition. Candidates include structured references, enumerations, labeled fields, compact measurement expressions, identifier/version forms and controlled condition fragments. Multiple start symbols let related forms share preparation without forcing one document-wide grammar. Lexer callbacks, `Token.update()` and `Token.new_borrow_pos()` can attach or normalize lexical readings while retaining original coordinates.

**Value and placement.** This extends the existing threshold/literal approach upstream: identify what a span structurally contains before assigning conceptual meaning. Emit the exact source extent, recognized form, grammar identity and unresolved/unsupported outcome for later combination. Preserve original spelling alongside canonical values. Comprehensive characterization can combine lexical, linguistic and formal passes; it need not require every ordinary word to belong to a handcrafted grammar.

**Countercondition.** A simple anchored pattern remains appropriate for a genuinely flat cue. A grammar buys little if it merely relocates one regex; it matters when nested composition, typed components or competing readings become real requirements. Broad catch-all terminals can hide useful structure and absorb prose accidentally. Existing conservative unit nomination illustrates a meaningful boundary to retain or revise deliberately.

**Next question.** Which source families have repeated compositional structures that current atomization or field parsing leaves opaque, and which constituent roles would downstream consumers actually reuse? Assess recognition coverage and semantic usefulness independently. Lark 1.3.1 has no released `Lark.scan()` method: region discovery must use existing source structure, lexical cues or another explicit nominator. Installed `lark.py` confirms lexer-only terminal retention; [tagged API source](https://github.com/lark-parser/lark/blob/1.3.1/lark/lark.py) establishes the configuration boundary.

### 2. Feed retained linguistic and formal evidence into a typed-token grammar

**Mechanism.** A custom `lark.lexer.Lexer` can consume an application-defined input object and emit typed Lark tokens; `%declare` names externally supplied terminal classes. Alternatively, LALR `parse_interactive()` accepts explicitly fed tokens. A bridge could classify retained spaCy tokens/spans and already recognized formulas, references or quantities as grammar symbols while retaining original extents and evidence references.

**Current use and opportunity.** Current math parsing consumes text with dynamic Earley lexing. The [annotation view](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/ingest/annotation_views.py) separately reconstructs native spaCy views from retained columns without retokenization. [predicate_frames.py](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/predicate_frames.py) already uses lexical, phrase and dependency matchers. The proposed bridge would consume those established products rather than rerun annotation or flatten everything into new normalized text. It could recognize bounded combinations such as a scoped condition plus a formal comparison, or a naming phrase plus a formula component.

**Value and placement.** Lark would own grammatical composition of typed evidence; spaCy would still own linguistic annotations and dependency evidence. Existing semantic owners would interpret the composed candidate. This is a possible reorganization of upstream characterization, not merely another math extension.

**Countercondition.** A linear token grammar does not naturally replace arbitrary dependency-graph matching. spaCy's Matcher/DependencyMatcher may express a case more directly. One token type per emitted item also requires a deliberate policy for overlapping spans, alternative classifications and formula boundaries; premature classification can erase the very ambiguity the grammar should retain. Emitting a token with an object value does not automatically preserve every original object's identity and metadata through persistence.

**Next question.** For one genuinely compositional source family, does a typed-token grammar clarify relationships that native spaCy matchers leave scattered, while preserving exact source pieces and alternatives? Compare complete alternatives rather than assuming a bridge is needed. Lark's [1.3.1 custom-input example](https://github.com/lark-parser/lark/blob/1.3.1/examples/advanced/custom_lexer.py) and installed `parser_frontends.py:_wrap_lexer` establish this extension route. Custom lexing replaces dynamic lexical exploration; it is not a postprocessor inserted into the current dynamic lexer. The old and newer custom-lexer interfaces also differ, including TextSlice handling, so choose an explicit supported interface rather than copy an arbitrary example.

### 3. Organize grammar families and typed output around recurring structures

**Mechanism.** Templates express separated lists and repeated delimited forms. `%import` namespaces shared grammar components; `%extend` adds alternatives; `%override` specializes imported definitions. `merge_transformers()` connects imported namespaces to their handlers. Aliases and tree shaping can name domain-relevant syntactic forms. `ast_utils.Ast`, `AsList`, `WithMeta` and `create_transformer()` can construct typed dataclass ASTs after parsing.

**Value and placement.** A family of recognizable formal fragments could share terminals and list/range/reference structures while keeping independent entry points and interpretation owners. Typed ASTs could replace repeated child-position unpacking at the characterization boundary and make syntax distinctions explicit before later relationship assembly. Current SymPy-plus-extension assembly is already a useful example; it need not be rewritten solely to exercise imports.

**Countercondition.** Importing grammar does not import its `%ignore` policy. Extensions change all references to the extended symbol and can introduce ambiguity. AST generation is convention-based constructor dispatch, not schema validation; aliases, optional placeholders, field order and constructors must agree. A shared grammar package earns its lifecycle only when real consumers share structure. One central universal grammar would couple unrelated source families.

**Next question.** Which current and intended recognizers share stable syntax rather than coincidentally similar spelling? Which source metadata must survive into their typed output? Postparse transformation is preferable where provenance is required: parse-time LALR transformers restrict metadata callbacks and `Discard`, and Earley does not support that embedded-transformer route. Installed `ast_utils.py:create_transformer` also shows that generated callbacks can overwrite methods on the supplied transformer. The [tagged grammar documentation](https://github.com/lark-parser/lark/blob/1.3.1/docs/grammar.md) and [AST implementation](https://github.com/lark-parser/lark/blob/1.3.1/lark/ast_utils.py) are focused follow-up sources.

### 4. Preserve ambiguity as evidence; investigate forests only for a concrete consumer

**Mechanism.** Earley supports selected trees, explicit `_ambig` trees or a shared packed parse forest. `ForestVisitor`, `ForestTransformer` and `TreeForestTransformer` allow direct traversal, filtering and processing of shared derivations. Rule and terminal priorities, global `priority="normal"`, `"invert"` or `None`, and custom prioritizers offer distinct preference controls. `ordered_sets=True` supports stable traversal order.

**Value and placement.** Current explicit trees already preserve correlated mathematical alternatives. A forest could support upstream inventories of possible structures without enumerating complete parses. Retained contextual evidence could later eliminate incompatible readings, while remaining alternatives stay inspectable. This is especially relevant when comprehensive characterization should expose uncertainty rather than silently select a plausible reading.

**Countercondition.** Grammar priorities express structural preference, not confidence, truth or source authority. Inverting priorities affects terminals as well as rules. The internal `ForestSumVisitor` aggregates derivation priorities; it is not a general semantic ranking engine. Forest transformation does not automatically reproduce ordinary tree filtering, shaping and position propagation. Cycles and sharing require explicit semantics, and cache reuse assumes suitable pure transformations. `CollapseAmbiguities` can expand combinations dramatically.

**Next question.** Does any intended consumer actually need shared alternatives that explicit trees cannot represent adequately? If so, what is the smallest source-grounded projection it needs, and how will correlations, cycles and positions survive? Retain explicit trees if they satisfy the supported workload. Installed `parsers/earley_forest.py` and the [tagged forest documentation](https://github.com/lark-parser/lark/blob/1.3.1/docs/forest.rst) establish the representation differences; no performance improvement is claimed.

### 5. Make provenance a property of the representation, not an assumption about trees

**Mechanism.** Positional propagation, container spans and `WithMeta` support source-aware structures. `TextSlice` retains original-document coordinates under basic/contextual lexing. `lex(dont_ignore=True)` exposes ignored lexical material. These can compose with original source retention and explicit coordinate mappings.

**Current boundary and value.** [core/text_structure.py:syntax_structure](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/core/text_structure.py) projects already prepared positioned math trees, adding the recognized view's origin. [_tree.py](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/math/_tree.py) derives names from source extents rather than printed leaves. [native.py](../../../../../python/corpus-structure/src/corpus_structure/methods/math/native.py) explicitly serializes token start/end positions and node extents, then restores them for normalization without recognition. Those are valuable existing invariants for broader characterization.

**Countercondition.** TextSlice is not available to the current partial-slice dynamic Earley route, and constructing lexical state for a late slice processes preceding text for line accounting. Source spans, zero-copy selection and incremental reparsing are separate capabilities. `keep_all_tokens=True` does not retain `%ignore` material. Experimental `Reconstructor` regenerates grammatical text, not exact original trivia or spelling.

Installed `tree.py` and `lexer.py` confirm the addendum's consequential corrections: `Tree.copy()` shares children and omits root metadata; deep-copying a Tree shares its metadata object; deep-copying or pickling a Token omits end positions. The current explicit native serializer preserves offsets, so these observations do **not** establish a durable native-product bug. `MathResult.__getstate__` converts the macro mapping but does not implement token-coordinate preservation; direct pickle transport remains a separate boundary to inspect if complete token positions become a consumed promise. Python `match Tree(...)` is supported through `__match_args__`; `Tree.match()` is absent. Top-down traversal is preorder depth-first and may revisit shared objects.

**Next question.** What exact positional information does each characterization consumer need: absolute offsets, local offsets, line/column, container extent, discontinuous pieces or retained spelling? Preserve that contract explicitly across transforms and transport. The source-supported `__lark_meta__()` hook is a lower-level possibility, not a reason to bypass the documented AST metadata route.

### 6. Improve diagnostic characterization and reuse preparation selectively

**Mechanism.** `UnexpectedCharacters`, `UnexpectedToken`, `UnexpectedEOF`, `get_context()` and `match_examples()` distinguish failure classes and support useful explanations. LALR interactive state exposes accepted terminal types and controlled recovery. Strict LALR construction plus optional interegular can diagnose supported grammar/regex collisions. `get_terminal()` exposes actual compiled terminal patterns.

**Value and placement.** Preserve recognized constituents and explicit unsupported regions alongside failures, so unsuccessful formal interpretation does not erase lexical characterization. Grammar-aware diagnostics can explain why a proposed unit was not recognized. Current math classification and limited punctuation retry are existing policies to assess, not replace automatically.

**Countercondition and next question.** Injected or skipped tokens describe a repaired reading, not an exact reading of original source. Would a consumer benefit from a separately labeled repair candidate, or only the original failure? Recovery is LALR-specific, whereas current mathematical parsing is Earley. `accepts()` yields syntactic token classes, not semantic completions. Interactive advancement is not edit-aware incremental reparsing. Keep unrepaired evidence and repair provenance distinct if this route is investigated.

Preparation should follow grammar/configuration validity and actual operation ownership. Current lazy parser dictionaries and field-grammar caches already reuse construction. LALR cache/save/load/standalone generation are potential deployment choices for expanded deterministic families; they do not serialize current Earley preparation. A postlexer with Earley `lexer="auto"` changes selection to basic; explicit dynamic-plus-postlex is rejected. `always_accept` also affects terminal visibility. Therefore, normalization or indentation postprocessing cannot be added as behavior-neutral plumbing.

## Research handoff

The strongest combined direction is **source-aligned lexical and linguistic evidence → bounded grammar composition → typed positioned structures and alternatives → separately owned interpretation and conceptual assembly**. Investigate lexical/nonmath families and the typed-token/spaCy bridge first, because they could broaden upstream characterization materially. Investigate forests, recovery and deployment changes only when their specific consumer questions arise.

This follows semantic authority and composition principles while applying execution-fit judgment: share established preparation, consume retained annotations, preserve useful intermediate products and avoid a parser run, persistence layer or universal grammar per semantic distinction. A full target reorganization remains available if contextual assessment supports it; this discovery neither adopts one nor schedules production changes.
