# T1 notes (Location-Scale Families)

## Guesses
- Statement split (§10.2, §10.3). The unit holds a setup paragraph and a definition. I made two
  statements: `s_assume` (kind `assumption`, for "In this section, we assume ...") and `s_def`
  (`definition`). `assumption` versus `narrative` for the first paragraph is a guess. Because this
  probe fixes statement anchors to `"unit"`, both statements have the same anchor and cannot be
  told apart by anchor (in stored form their keys would collide: D1 §10.1 keys a statement by
  its anchor segments).
- Section scope (§10.4). "In this section, we assume that we fixed random variable Z" binds Z for
  the section: `e_Z` extent `section`, and the supposition carries `scope: section`. The
  guideline says to use "the section's first binding as the scope anchor"; the model-facing form
  only has the extent, so I could not record which binding is the anchor.
- "let X = a + b Z" inside a definition (§10.6). "let" says `supposed`; "what a definition
  stipulates" says `defined`. I used `defined` with role `definiens`.
- defined_as versus construction_formula (§10.3). The table lists both for definitions. I
  recorded both: `X defined_as a + b \, Z` and `location-scale family construction_formula
  a + b \, Z` with `base_variable` Z and `param_symbols` {location: a, scale: b}. This may
  double-count one fact.
- Symbol bindings of a and b (§10.7, D1 §6.1). They are introduced by the definition (`free`) but
  the last sentence names them as the location and scale parameters; I used `family_param`
  bound to the parameter entities.
- Parameter domains. "For a in R and b in (0, inf)" gave both `parameter_domain` claims
  (`defined`, role `restriction`) and `member` conditions on the definiens claims. Possibly
  redundant.
- `parameter_role` (§10.4). The role words are stated, so the canonical names are `location`
  and `scale`; I did not add `parameter_role` claims, reading the rule as needed only when the
  canonical name falls back to a symbol.
- The definiens claims' condition trees also reference the section premise `c_Z_values`; the
  guideline does not say whether a section-scoped supposition should appear in later trees.
- `has_parameter` claims are modality `defined` (stipulated in the definition).

## Possibly mis-rendered text
- "we assume that we fixed random variable \( Z \)": a missing article ("a random variable")
  or a missing "have"; probably the source's wording.
- "Definition" stands alone as a paragraph (a rendered heading/label of the definition box).
- Macros `\ms F`, `\P` and `\mathscr F` are copied as written; `\ms F` and `\mathscr F` denote
  the same thing in two notations.

## Not expressible (recorded as unsupported)
- The random experiment and probability space (Omega, F, P), the notation sentence for its
  components, and "Z defined on the probability space". The schema has no probability-space
  class or predicate.
- "The two-parameter family" (the count of parameters) has no predicate; it is implied by the
  two `has_parameter` claims and cited only as supporting evidence.
