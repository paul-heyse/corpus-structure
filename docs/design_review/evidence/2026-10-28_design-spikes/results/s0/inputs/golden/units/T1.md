## Text
[s1] As usual, our starting point is a random experiment, modeled by a probability space \((\Omega, \ms F, \P)\).
[s2] So \( \Omega \) is the set of outcomes, \( \ms F \) the collection of events, and \( \P \) the probability measure on the sample space \( (\Omega, \mathscr F) \).
[s3] In this section, we assume that we fixed random variable \( Z \) defined on the probability space, with values in \( \R \).
[s4] For \(a \in \R\) and \(b \in (0, \infty) \), let \(X = a + b \, Z\).
[s5] The two-parameter family of distributions associated with \(X\) is called the location-scale family associated with the given distribution of \(Z\).
[s6] Specifically, \(a\) is the location parameter and \(b\) the scale parameter.
