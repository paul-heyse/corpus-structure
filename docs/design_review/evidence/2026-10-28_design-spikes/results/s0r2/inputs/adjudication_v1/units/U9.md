## Text
[s1] Suppose that \(n \in \N_+\) and that \((X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n)\) is a sequence of independent variables, where \(X_i\) has the lognormal distribution with parameters \(\mu_i \in \R\) and \(\sigma_i \in (0, \infty)\) for \(i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}\).
[s2] Then \(\prod_{i=1}^n X_i\) has the lognormal distribution with parameters \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\) where \(\mu = \sum_{i=1}^n \mu_i\) and \(\sigma^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2\).
--- Details: ---
[s3] Again from definition [1], we can write \( X_i = e^{Y_i} \) where \(Y_i\) has the normal distribution with mean \(\mu_i\) and standard deviation \(\sigma_i\) for \(i \in \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}\) and where \((Y_1, Y_2, \ldots, Y_n)\) is an independent sequence.
[s4] Hence \(\prod_{i=1}^n X_i = \exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\right)\).
[s5] But \(\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i\) has the normal distribution with mean \(\sum_{i=1}^n \mu_i\) and variance \(\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2\).
