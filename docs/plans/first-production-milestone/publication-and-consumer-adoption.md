### Release boundary and cutover

Use the current release for unchanged meanings; prepare a new semantic release when the remaining
record/evidence/interpretation work changes those meanings. M1's release
manifest names English/statistics, PDF/selected native views, supported subsets of each family,
unsupported strata, operation/schema/checkpoint/tokenizer/configuration and splitter versions,
acceptance/calibration standing, resource bounds and source/recipe/cut lineage. A changed model
input, grouping or acceptance policy receives the corresponding new version and replay scope.
The manifest must distinguish a candidate-only subset from an accepted released one.

Migration is forward preparation and replay of selected source inputs into the new release, not
mutation of frozen predecessor observations, historical answers or grades. Preserve previous
accepted cuts for rollback. No parallel intake route, mandatory historical reader compatibility
or broad historical regrading is introduced. When the CPU policy is activated, selected M1 runs
must not reach current statistics `agent.opus` extraction as an implicit fallback. Existing
agent/evaluator workflows retain their original contracts outside that selected policy.

The implementer inventories current users of changed declared fields/projections and moves actual
publication, query, export, evaluator and reporting consumers in the same release transition.
Remove replaced M1-local flattening or duplicate interpretation paths once their consumers move.
Do not retire predecessor data or unrelated workflows merely to simplify a test. A new release
can exist without being default-selected while consumers and evidence are incomplete.

Any native change after frozen105 uses a new forward successor, preserving immutable predecessor
bytes, sidecar hashes, existing supported archive readers and the live native99 application.
AC improvement I03 separates fresh installation, predecessor-forward and current-head reauthoring
controls. Run full-chain installs serially on the dedicated cluster, and retain actual-catalog
collation drift detection. Retirement follows registered operations, generated bindings and archived
consumers as well as direct callers; do not remove live `complete_unit_result` or reference adjudication.

### Failure, interruption and recovery

Fail publication when release/source bindings changed, required evidence cannot resolve, a required
qualification is unresolved, record membership is inconsistent or policy admission fails. Preserve
the prepared candidate diagnostics; no partial knowledge write or selected pointer move may survive
failure. Retry/idempotence uses the existing operation/publication identity and receipt boundary,
not a second lease/cache system. A completed immutable report survives interruption; resume repeats
the smallest incomplete owned unit. Cancellation and fencing retain their typed meanings.

Changed context, withdrawn rejected evidence or a changed note can invalidate interpretation even
when the winner's proving anchors are unchanged. Use M1-10 complete computation dependencies to
recompute affected components/descendants before publication. The prior accepted result remains
inspectable and pinned while a new result is prepared. No automatic selection of incomplete output
or serving through a moving project default is permitted.

Focused checks cover a real five-family candidate batch, multi-role/multi-anchor preservation,
duplicate source occurrences, conflicting qualified records, invalid required evidence, atomic
rollback and duplicate publication receipts. Independently verify that changing a note or rejected
alternative changes the required replay scope. These checks establish publication mechanics;
actual extraction quality and final gate/adoption evidence remain M1-14.
